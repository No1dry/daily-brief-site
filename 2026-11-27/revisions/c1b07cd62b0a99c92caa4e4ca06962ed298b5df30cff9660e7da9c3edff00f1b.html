👍 5

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/24 08:00

Agent-orchestrated robot systems combine the general reasoning capability of multimodal agents with the precise physical control of learned visuomotor policies. Yet such systems often fail at the moment of handoff: a policy is reliable only from a limited set of start states, while the agent may invoke it outside this region. We call this region the policy's handoff boundary. Existing whole-task policies typically have handoff boundaries that are narrow and difficult for an agent to characterize, making reliable coordination challenging. We propose Boundary-Aware Policy Learning, which explicitly shapes and then expands the handoff boundary of a visuomotor policy. First, Boundary-Explicit Training fine-tunes the policy on contact segments with diverse start poses, yielding a wide and well-defined boundary that can be communicated to the agent through a simple handoff protocol. Second, Boundary-Expanding Self-Improvement retrains the policy on curated successful system rollouts, extending the boundary toward earlier stages of each task and allowing the agent to hand over sooner with fewer language-model calls. Across RoboTwin 2.0 and RoboDojo, our system outperforms the strongest baseline by over 26 percentage points, reaching 98.4% success in randomized RoboTwin 2.0 scenes, while further reducing its API cost and execution time by 41.3% and 37.5%, respectively. These results suggest that the key to robust agent-policy orchestration lies not only in improving either component individually, but also in explicitly shaping the boundary between them.

CARE-3D: Reinforcement Learning for Evidence Grounded Agentic Reasoning in Situated 3D Scenes

👍 1

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/23 08:00

Situated 3D reasoning requires embodied agents to interpret objects, scene geometry, and viewpoint dependent spatial relations. Existing methods learn grounded interactions from supervised traces, but largely imitate prescribed evidence acquisition patterns rather than optimizing the support that the returned evidence provides for the correct answer. Moreover, outcome rewards provide limited credit for intermediate tool choices and may leave infrequent tools underexplored. An effective agent should instead adaptively decide what to ground, which tool to invoke, and how to assess the utility of returned evidence. To this end, this paper presents CARE-3D, a reinforcement post-training framework for adaptive grounded reasoning. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on grounding instructions first establishes valid interactions, after which Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) optimizes the agent while keeping the 3D grounding module frozen. CARE-3D combines Counterfactual Attribution of Returned Evidence for evidence sensitive credit assignment, and Group Coverage Sampling to preserve tool coverage. Experiments on MSQA and Beacon3D show consistent gains over SFT and GRPO using only answer and format rewards, especially in spatial reasoning and grounding answer coherence. Our code is available at https://github.com/xiaoqian-UQ/CARE-3D{CARE-3D}.

When Every Rollout Fails: Splicing Learner-Conditioned Options into Long-Horizon Agent RL

👍 3

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/25 08:00

Group-based reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards often yields all-fail groups on long-horizon agent tasks: every sampled trajectory receives zero reward, leaving group-relative methods without a reward-based policy gradient. A common remedy supplies complete solutions from a stronger model, but full trajectories are expensive and expose the learner to long off-policy histories. We introduce SPLICE, which constructs local training targets from failed current-policy trajectories online. For each all-fail group, a proposal model selects a splice point in a current-policy trajectory and generates several bounded options from that point. We estimate each option's value using repeated current-policy continuations, convert positive estimates into a return-weighted local target, and fit the current policy on the options. We evaluate SPLICE across two policy families on two long-horizon agent tasks, Software Engineering with SWE-bench Verified and Deep Research with BrowseComp-Plus. SPLICE outperforms GRPO and full-trajectory guidance baselines in all four settings, improving over GRPO by 2.8--9.8 percentage points. On initially failed SWE tasks, it raises GRPO's graduation rate by 10.8 percentage points. Further experiments show that SPLICE improves performance while reducing costly proposal-model computation.

Benchmarks judge language-model agents mainly by whether they reach the correct final state, not by how they got there. We present DetourBench, which constrains the environment until that minimum is computable. Each task is a finite, partially observable transition system with unit-cost accesses whose resources become globally addressable after discovery. Exhaustive search yields the full-information minimum action count L_0 with a replayable witness; from graph-recoverable terminal states it also yields exact residual work R_T, which separates an episode that stopped one access short from one that wandered, while an irreversible wrong commitment is recorded as having no finite residual distance rather than assigned one.
