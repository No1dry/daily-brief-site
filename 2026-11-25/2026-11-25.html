Embodied AI Fails the Mirror Test: How a Simple Mirror Breaks Robot Policies

👍 15

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/24 08:00

Can a simple mirror expose the limitations of today's robotic manipulation policies? Inspired by the mirror self-recognition test in animal cognition, we investigate whether robot policies can reliably manipulate physical objects in scenes containing their reflections. We introduce RoboDojo-Mirror, a benchmark built on RoboDojo that preserves task objectives, success criteria, and policy checkpoints while comparing a mirror-free baseline with single-mirror, dual-mirror, and mirrored-tabletop conditions. Across five policies spanning Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, and agentic policies, every mirror condition reduces aggregate success rates: averaged over the three conditions, the drop ranges from 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points across policies, and it reaches 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Because a mirror is also a physical obstacle, we compare it against geometrically matched non-reflective panels; 50--88\% of mirror-specific failures occur without recorded robot--panel contact, so direct collisions alone do not explain the degradation. Trajectory and contact analyses instead reveal recurring behaviors consistent with phantom targeting of reflected objects and spatial mislocalization of real ones. Targeted diagnostics further characterize grasping near reflections, policy-dependent pushing progress, and physical-target interaction under main-camera occlusion. Real-robot experiments with π_{0.5} and FastWAM also show lower overall success rates under the upright-mirror and mirrored-tabletop conditions. These findings reveal a shared vulnerability to reflective environments across diverse robot policies and motivate reflection-aware evaluation of embodied intelligence. Code is available at https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror.

中文介绍 构建 RoboDojo-Mirror 基准，考察含镜面反射场景下机器人操作策略的鲁棒性，覆盖无镜、单镜、双镜与镜面桌面条件。五种 VLA、世界动作模型与智能体策略在所有镜面条件下成功率均下降，平均降幅 10.8–56.7 个百分点，镜面桌面达 62.5 点；50–88% 的镜面特有失败无接触，表现为对反射物体的「幻影」瞄准与真实物体空间误定位。

ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control

👍 44

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/23 08:00

Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents. Each member reasons and acts for a configurable control group, such as an arm with its gripper or the torso and chassis, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution. However, different robot components often have physical dependencies that require coordination. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents. This allows independent work to progress concurrently while supporting tightly coupled manipulation when necessary. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across all eight Collaborative settings, ACAP also achieves lower mean time cost than Fixed Delegation, where only member agents can control their assigned groups. An additional 90-rollout study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We further demonstrate mobile manipulation using the same member interface for chassis and torso control. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

中文介绍 提出异步编程智能体框架 ACAP，将单机器人拆分为多个持久化智能体，各自负责可配置控制组（如机械臂+夹爪、躯干+底盘），由 hyper-agent 负责任务分配、依赖协调与控制权动态切换。在 25 种任务/平台配置、72 次真实环境中，任务均衡耗时较单智能体 Direct Policy 降低 28.2%，8 个协作场景均优于固定委派。

Beam Model Technical Report

👍 14

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/19 08:00

Beam is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 501.3B total parameters and 23.4B active parameters, specifically built and optimized for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads. Beam delivers frontier level reasoning, while requiring 3-4x less inference compute than many models of its class. Pretrained on 23.8T diverse, curated, high-quality tokens, Beam features a 1M-token context window achieved through a novel two-stage midtraining phase, built on verifiable, correctness-anchored data. Context-Grounded Skill Internalization for Vision-Language Agents

👍 2

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/23 08:00

Skill internalization offers a way for agents to learn useful behaviors from skills and use them without explicit guidance in context at inference time.
