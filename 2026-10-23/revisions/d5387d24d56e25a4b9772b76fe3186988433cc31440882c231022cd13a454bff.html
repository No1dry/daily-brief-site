FrontierCS 2: A Benchmark for Computer Science R&D

👍 2

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/21 08:00

Can LLM agents make contributions to frontier computer science R&D? We introduce FrontierCS 2, a benchmark of 48 open-ended tasks. We take a paper and the code its authors released and delete the paper's contribution. The deleted code becomes the human reference that sets the bar. Earlier open-ended benchmarks give each agent a problem statement and grade one program after the run. FrontierCS 2 places the agent inside a research environment. The agent inherits a working repository and may submit a patch at any point. For each patch, a judge returns a development-suite score but keeps the evaluator, the held-out final suite and the reference private, and grades the final submission on that suite against the reference. The tasks span 13 domains, from machine-learning systems and high-performance computing to databases, security and robotics. Beyond these 48 tasks, FrontierCS 2 keeps a private set of more than 100 tasks, to be announced in the future. We evaluate 12 agents. The best one is Claude Opus 5.5, passing 58% of its runs. No run passes on 16 tasks. A higher score is not the same as a pass: most runs that beat the reference on the combined final score still fall short on some metric or workload. On a shared capability scale for agents and tasks, the median task is roughly as difficult as what the strongest agent can handle.

中文介绍 提出 FrontierCS 2，评估 LLM 智能体能否对前沿计算机科学研发做出实质贡献。取论文及其开源代码并删除论文贡献，删去的代码作为人类参考基线；智能体在可运行仓库中工作并随时提交 patch，仅返回开发集分数，最终由私有留出测试集与参考对比评分。48 个开放任务覆盖 13 个领域，评测 12 个智能体，最佳 Claude Opus 5.5 通过 58% 运行，16 个任务无任何通过。

👍 11

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/21 08:00

Vision-language-action (VLA) models achieve strong performance across embodied manipulation tasks by unifying visual understanding, language conditioning, and action generation. However, long-horizon tasks can involve non-Markovian dependencies: successful decisions may require retaining task progress and past interaction outcomes, while partial observability and execution errors compound over time. Existing work has begun to address these challenges by augmenting foundation-model policies with memory and by introducing hierarchical architectures that separate high-level planning from low-level execution. RoboCasa-Shift: Stage-Aware Robustness Evaluation of Vision-Language-Action Policies in Long-Horizon Household Manipulation

👍 2

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/20 08:00

Vision-language-action (VLA) robustness is commonly summarized by aggregate task success, which obscures how individual executions change under distribution shifts and where failures emerge in long-horizon tasks. We introduce RoboCasa-Shift, a diagnostic robustness benchmark built on RoboCasa365 that combines controlled single-factor shifts, matched clean-perturbed rollouts in which each pair shares the same underlying settings and differs only in the target shift, and simulator-grounded stage-aware diagnostics. We evaluate GR00T N1.5 on four long-horizon tasks that pass a predefined clean-performance gate under four perturbation factors and three calibrated severity levels, including conditions that drive the evaluated policy to complete task failure, totaling 1,040 formal rollouts. Robustness responses vary substantially across factors and tasks, and increasing severity does not always produce monotonic degradation. For example, one camera condition yields ΔSR=0 despite 8 of 80 matched episodes changing outcome. Stage-aware analysis further shows that the same end-to-end degradation can correspond to different execution-depth profiles. Camera L_2 and Gaussian blur L_1 both yield ΔSR=0.188, yet their first-stage failure rates are 19.0\% and 36.4\%, respectively. Our findings suggest that VLA robustness should be viewed as a multi-dimensional property of execution rather than a single end-to-end performance measure, motivating evaluation protocols that jointly capture outcome degradation, paired outcome sensitivity, and failure progression. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/HaochengWang23/RoboCasa-Shift.

Skeleton-Grounded Latent Action Model for Robot World Models

👍 1

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/22 08:00

Robot world models simulate how environments evolve in response to robot actions. Latent action models enable learning dynamics from videos by encoding visual transitions as proxy actions. However, reconstruction-driven latent actions mix robot motion with object responses and other scene changes, complicating their correspondence with robot controls. We propose SkeLAM, a skeleton-guided latent action model for controllable robot world modeling. SkeLAM decomposes latent actions into a kinematic component grounded by robot skeleton supervision and a residual component retaining complementary visual dynamics. Cross-modal swap reconstruction encourages RGB-derived and skeleton-derived kinematic representations to be interchangeable during video reconstruction. The world model incorporates both components through separate conditioning pathways, while branch dropout reduces its dependence on future-derived residual information. A lightweight adapter maps robot actions to the kinematic space, and subsequent world-model fine-tuning adapts generation to these predicted conditions. At inference, the model generates future observations from the current observation and robot actions. On DROID, SkeLAM reduces end-effector ADE by 19.5% and LPIPS by 11.1% compared with DreamDojo. Motion probing supports kinematic specialization, while qualitative results demonstrate zero-shot human-to-robot motion transfer in generated rollouts. Policy evaluation shows agreement between predicted success rates and those measured in real robot executions.

中文介绍 提出 SkeLAM，将潜动作分解为由机器人骨架监督的运动学分量与保留其余视觉动态的残差分量，通过跨模态交换重建使 RGB 与骨架导出的运动学表征可互换，世界模型以独立条件通路融合两者并用 branch dropout 降低对残差信息的依赖。在 DROID 上较 DreamDojo 将末端执行器 ADE 降低 19.5%、LPIPS 降低 11.1%，并展示零样本人体到机器人动作迁移。

Retrieval Augmented Generation Compositionality

👍 28

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/21 08:00

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds large language models in external knowledge. Modern pipelines combine embedding, chunking, retrieval, reranking, query augmentation, context compression, and agentic verification, yet these techniques are typically evaluated one at a time. It remains unclear whether their isolated gains survive composition, whether retrieval quality and answer faithfulness favor the same design choices, and whether conclusions from small corpora hold at large scale. We pre-declare three hypotheses and introduce LASAGNA, an open-source framework for controlled RAG evaluation. We test 450 configurations across eight benchmarks and corpora ranging from 5K to 200K documents. Three findings emerge. First, individually strong techniques from different phases of a RAG pipeline do not necessarily compose: three retrieval techniques predict a +47% combined improvement but deliver only +17% when used together. Second, component gains stack differently for retrieval and faithfulness, and optimizing one can hurt the other. Third, the best composition changes as the corpus grows: the combination that wins on a small corpus may not remain best at large scale. These findings show that gains measured in isolation are not reliable predictors of realistic performance, and that new RAG techniques should be tested with the full surrounding stack. We call on researchers to do so and supply LASAGNA to simplify these experiments.

中文介绍 系统研究 RAG 各环节技术叠加后的组合效果。开源框架 LASAGNA 在 8 个基准、5K 至 200K 文档语料上测试 450 种配置，发现：单项技术增益不必然可组合，三项检索技术的预测增益为 +47%，实际仅 +17%；检索质量与忠实度的最优设计取向不同；最优组合随语料规模变化。主张新方法须在完整流水线中评估。
