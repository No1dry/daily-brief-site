PR-OPD: Privileged Representation On-policy Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning

👍 28

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 09/29 08:00

Language-model agents are usually trained by reinforcement learning from one reward per episode, and privileged self-distillation enriches it by letting the same policy, given a skill, teach its skill-free self through token probabilities. However, we identify two phenomena that question this channel. Invisible Advantage: a skill in context lifts WebShop success from 42.2% to 56.2%, yet changes the probabilities of fewer than a quarter of the sampled tokens. Much to Align: a skill changes the hidden states of over 80% of response tokens, in a way that linear probes can trace back to the specific skill. To exploit this, we propose Privileged Representation On-policy Self-Distillation (PR-OPD). After a GRPO warm start, the policy writes a hindsight skill for each trajectory, re-reads its own responses with that skill as a stop-gradient teacher, and aligns its projected hidden states to the teacher's at every layer alongside the reward objective, with no external skill library, separate teacher, or inference overhead. On ALFWorld and WebShop with two backbones, PR-OPD achieves the best overall results in every setting, improving over GRPO by up to 4.7 points in ALFWorld success and 14.0 points in WebShop accuracy. Code is available at https://github.com/balibata/PR-OPD.

RSIPO: Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization for Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Skill-Policy Co-Evolution

👍 2

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/11 08:00

Recent focus in agentic reinforcement learning (RL) seeks to improve credit assignment in long-horizon tasks. One promising direction combines reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) and on-policy self-distillation (OPSD), integrating trajectory-level advantages with dense token-level supervision. This line of work often conditions a teacher model on privileged context, such as agentic skills, to score tokens generated by the student policy. Despite their progress, they largely overlook the on-policy evolution of agentic skills and the reliability of teacher supervision. Towards this end, we introduce Recursive Self-Improving Policy Optimization (RSIPO), a self-evolving agentic RL framework that enables agentic skills and the policy model to co-evolve throughout the training process. Specifically, RSIPO enables the policy itself to extract skills from completed trajectories and explicitly optimizes its skill-generation capability through trajectory replay. It further employs empirical Bayes shrinkage to dynamically calibrate the student's trust in teacher scores, accounting for the fact that skill-enhanced teacher guidance can sometimes be less reliable. Experiments with Qwen-family models on embodied interaction and search-based agentic tasks demonstrate that RSIPO outperforms all the compared baseline approaches. With Qwen2.5-7B as the base model, RSIPO achieves an average success rate of 98.6\% on ALFWorld, yielding absolute improvements of 2.5\% and 17.4\% over the second-best baseline and standard GRPO, respectively.

ClawLoop: Self-Improving Agents via Harness-Native Training Loop

👍 3

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 10/09 08:00

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has driven their deployment toward general-purpose harness environments, where LLM-based agents operate in sandbox workspaces and interact directly with file systems and shells. While numerous post-training pipelines exist for tool-use agents, their counterparts for harness-based agents remain scarce and are predominantly one-shot and teacher-dependent: a stronger external model synthesizes tasks and collects trajectories, the target agent is trained once, and evaluation results are consumed by human researchers rather than fed back into the loop. Such pipelines are inherently bounded by the capability of the external model and the scope of human prior, leaving no room for self-improvement. To address these limitations, we propose ClawLoop, a framework that enables self-improving without any external teacher model and with minimal prior dependencies. ClawLoop composes three phases into a self-sustaining cycle: Task Synthesis, which generates a diverse pool of training tasks via two complementary construction routines, applies diagnosis-conditioned difficulty growth followed by both agent-free and agent-based task validation; Training, which updates the agent via SFT and/or RL; and Diagnosis and Feedback, which analyzes the agent's rollout behaviors to produce a capability analysis report that will be used as feedback for task synthesis in the next iteration. Using Qwen3.6-27B as the loop model, ClawLoop yields consistent improvements on SkillsBench, Claw-Eval-Live, and ClawGym-Bench. The iteratively refined tasks are further transferred to smaller-scale Qwen3.5-4B and 9B models with gains across all benchmarks, validating data quality independently of the loop model.
