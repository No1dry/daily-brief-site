WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency

👍 22

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/23 08:00

AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce WorldLeague, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of 60 simulated robot tasks by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in WorldSeek, rearrange objects in WorldShape, and adapt to changes in WorldShift. Tying these goals to a single control interface limits which AI systems can be compared. In WorldLeague, vision-language models (VLMs) choose targets and motions, while vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs) use their native controls. For each of six capability dimensions, we measure how much progress a system makes toward its goals (Interactive Score) and how often it achieves them (physical success rate). Together, these measures form the system's Physical Agency Profile. Among 13 representative AI systems, Claude Fable 5.2 achieves 13 of the 60 goals and earns the highest mean Interactive Score (28.58/100). Fable leads in four dimensions, while GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tie for first on rule-following tasks. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8\% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion. Project Webpage: https://xyangzhou.github.io/WorldLeague

中文介绍 提出 WorldLeague 基准，用 60 个仿真机器人任务从轨迹衡量物理目标进展，分 WorldSeek 探索、WorldShape 重排、WorldShift 适应三类，VLM 选目标与运动、VLA 与 WAM 用原生控制。13 个系统中 Claude Fable 5.2 完成 13 项且平均交互分最高（28.58/100），并揭示 VLM 答对最终问题却仍在 71.8% 回合交互分为零的差距。

A Glimpse of Training VLMs as Foundation Models for Robotic Manipulation

👍 23

HuggingFace Trending Papers · 11/22 08:00

Recently, GPT-6 Astra demonstrates strong generalization in robot control, yet limited disclosure of its training leaves open how such capabilities can be reproduced. We introduce nanoAstra, an approach to training vision-language models (VLMs) for generalizable manipulation that combines semantic action modeling with capability-preserving supervised fine-tuning (CP-SFT). nanoAstra represents robot behavior as semantic steps composed of reusable, parameterized action primitives, guided by explicit progress descriptions. Meanwhile, CP-SFT uses an explicit robot-specific system prompt to constrain manipulation outputs to a structured action space, aiming to focus adaptation on robot-specific contexts and promote specialization while minimizing interference with broader pretrained capabilities. With this formulation, we train nanoAstra on more than 5,000 hours of robot data together with general VQA data. On RoboTwin 2.0's randomized benchmark, nanoAstra-9B achieves a 17.8% zero-shot success rate, substantially outperforming advanced VLA and WAM baselines as well as similarly sized VLMs. With inference faster than the corresponding motion execution on a single H20 GPU, our model supports action-chunk-like streaming execution that overlaps inference with motion, reducing the robot's idle time from 35.0% to 6.9%.

中文介绍 为复现 GPT-6 Astra 式机器人控制能力，提出 nanoAstra：将机器人行为表示为可复用参数化动作原语组成的语义步骤，并配合能力保持监督微调 CP-SFT，用机器人专用系统提示约束输出到结构化动作空间。在 5000 余小时机器人数据与通用 VQA 上训练，RoboTwin 2.0 零样本成功率 17.8%，单卡 H20 推理快于运动执行，机器人空闲时间从 35.0% 降至 6.9%。
